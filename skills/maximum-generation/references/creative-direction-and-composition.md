# Creative Direction and Composition

## Direction record

Create a concise record before implementation. It should answer what the person should feel, remember, notice first, explore next, and experience as distinctive. Add one deliberate constraint; constraints prevent a broad request from dissolving into default patterns.

| Field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Audience and context | Who is using this, in what moment, and what do they need to feel or accomplish? |
| Experience promise | A single sentence about the desired lived experience, not a list of features. |
| Remembered moment | The scene, interaction, or transition that should remain after use. |
| Focal structure | Primary focal point, secondary detail, contrast, and a discovery path. |
| Distinctive constraint | A rule that prevents generic output, such as one material language, one interaction metaphor, or a defined mood. |
| First seconds | What appears, what is understood, and why the person continues. |
| Deliberate omissions | What is excluded to preserve clarity, performance, or mood. |

## Composition checks

For visual pages and worlds, inspect foreground, middle ground, background, focal hierarchy, negative space, scale, rhythm, depth, contrast, sightlines, and discovery paths. For non-spatial experiences, inspect information hierarchy, pacing, task sequence, progressive disclosure, feedback cadence, and recovery paths.

> Prefer authored randomness to uniform randomness. Variation should reinforce a readable structure rather than erase it.

## Weak-to-improved example

| Weak direction | Why it fails | Improved direction |
|---|---|---|
| “Make a voxel game with terrain, trees, mining, and inventory.” | It lists objects and features but makes no promise about experience or composition. | “Build a compact dusk expedition where a distant cairn draws the player through quiet cedar clearings; mining should feel weighty, and each turn should reveal one intentional landmark.” |
| “Make a dashboard that looks modern.” | “Modern” has no user outcome, hierarchy, or rejection criteria. | “Build a calm project-control surface for a busy student: the next commitment is obvious in three seconds, deep planning stays secondary, and the visual language avoids dashboard-card clutter.” |

## Failure signals

Rework the direction if it could describe any generic product, has no focal hierarchy, adds features without a user experience purpose, or requires unbounded assets and effects to become interesting.
