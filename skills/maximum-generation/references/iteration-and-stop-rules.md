# Iteration and Stop Rules

## Regenerate or repair when

Trigger a focused iteration when an explicit requirement fails, an observation contradicts the direction, a selected dimension is below its threshold, the first impression is weak, composition is empty, interaction feedback is absent, output is visibly repetitive, a generic pattern remains unexplained, or a change caused a regression.

Repair the smallest element that explains the weakness. Examples include one layout region, one interaction transition, one material family, one landmark, one asset, or one wording hierarchy. Rebuild the artifact only when its core direction is wrong or targeted repairs cannot preserve coherence.

## Stop when

Stop when hard requirements and relevant checks pass, quality evidence meets the declared threshold, remaining work has diminishing value, further work threatens clarity/accessibility/performance, or the stated time/resource boundary is reached. Stop as **partial** when observation is unavailable or a required weakness remains; name the next safe improvement.

## Report distinction

| Terminal label | Use when |
|---|---|
| Verified | Relevant required checks and evidence-backed quality review pass. |
| Partial | A useful result exists, but required evidence or a quality criterion remains incomplete. |
| Blocked | A required dependency, access, decision, or capability is unavailable. |
| Deferred | A lower-priority improvement is intentionally postponed with a reason. |

## Avoid these loops

Do not change multiple unrelated dimensions at once, keep iterating to chase an unbounded “perfect” goal, mistake more effects for better atmosphere, or continue after the user’s actual outcome is already served.
