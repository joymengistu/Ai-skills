# Evidence-Grounded Professional Judgment Contract

Professional judgment is **contextual intentionality under constraints**. It is a decision about fitness for a user, task, audience, content, environment, and risk—not a universal beauty score, vendor imitation, or claim of perfection.

## Judgment frame

Before reviewing an artifact, record the user outcome, audience, device or environment, realistic content, primary task, constraints, reference authority, failure cost, and requested quality bar. Translate adjectives such as *premium*, *modern*, *clean*, *professional*, or *beautiful* into observable criteria. Treat references as principles to decompose, never as permission to copy private layouts, assets, tokens, or hidden implementations.

## Evidence layers

| Layer | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Requirements and audience alignment | Usability or production readiness |
| Craft | Coherence of visual and structural details with realistic content | Accessibility, behavior, or task success |
| Usability | Representative people or task tests can complete intended flows | Universal preference or long-term value |
| Accessibility | Relevant perception, operation, understanding, and recovery paths were checked | All disabilities or every assistive technology |
| Resilience | Loading, empty, error, interruption, recovery, and performance behavior observed | Unseen production conditions |
| Human value | Reduced confusion, effort, anxiety, or friction without manipulation | Universal lovability or superiority |

Never infer a higher evidence layer from a lower one. A polished screenshot is craft evidence; a passing build is build-health evidence; a positive aesthetic review does not prove usability, accessibility, or backend correctness.

## Review dimensions

Choose only dimensions relevant to the task and record evidence, confidence, and limitations for each: outcome clarity, hierarchy, content reality, alignment, spacing, typography, density, interaction clarity, consistency, accessibility, performance/resilience, restraint, character, trust/transparency, and human perception. Keep hard blockers separate from averages. A score may summarize a dimension only after observations exist; it must never erase a critical failure or missing evidence.

## Judgment procedure

1. Define the decision and its context before scoring.
2. Inspect the real artifact with realistic content and the relevant viewport, input method, states, and reference.
3. Separate deterministic checks, expert observations, independent critique, user/task evidence, and unknowns.
4. Identify the highest-impact mismatch or blocker and its likely user cost. Prefer three high-leverage repairs over a long preference list.
5. Preserve intentional unusual choices when they serve the stated task or reference. Remove or quiet complexity only when it does not improve comprehension, action, feedback, accessibility, trust, recovery, or purposeful character.
6. Re-review after changes, including regression checks for hierarchy, content, accessibility, performance, and reference fidelity where relevant.
7. Stop when the acceptance bar is evidenced, the budget or authority boundary is reached, the direction is structurally wrong, or further iteration has diminishing value.

## Reporting contract

Return the context and definition of quality, evidence and confidence by dimension, critical blockers, three repairs, what should remain unchanged, not-assessable properties, tradeoffs, and the next validation step. Mark claims `verified`, `partial`, `unverified`, `deferred`, `blocked`, or `needs_review`. State whether each conclusion comes from automated checking, direct runtime observation, expert judgment, independent critique, or human review.

## Hard boundaries

Professional taste cannot override safety, privacy, authorization, truthfulness, accessibility, completion, or user intent. It does not authorize copying another product or claiming measured improvement without matched evidence. Screenshot-only evidence cannot establish hidden behavior, unseen responsive states, performance, semantic accessibility, or production readiness.
